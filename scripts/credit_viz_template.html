L1 is the error between credit shares (0 = exact, 2 = disjoint). The score is taken against each read-out's matched removal target: leave-one-out for VDN, QMIX, COMA, MAPPO and our sitting-out critic, Shapley for our coalition estimator. "Team reacts" grades every method against leave-one-out where the remaining agents re-plan without the removed one (the truth our sitting-out critic is built for). "Step r" is the Pearson correlation between the read-out and the per-step counterfactual it estimates (level-1 fidelity). The two reference rows need no training: equal shares for everyone, and a perfect per-step critic (the semantic gap).

What to look for

  • The semantic gap. Choose a slack enabler. A pulls the lever long before B and C reach their goals, so if A did nothing for one step it would just pull a step later. VDN, QMIX and COMA ask exactly that one-step question, so their A row stays close to zero. Removing A, however, loses the whole reward: the true-credit heatmap gives A a full third, landing at the lever pull. Even a perfect one-step critic misses the enabler.
  • Delay. Move from d = 0 to d = 50. The payout marker moves right, away from the actions that caused it, while the removal truth stays the same.
  • Bystanders. D and E's rows show leakage: credit given to agents who could be removed without losing anything.
  • Our methods. Pick Ours: trained with sitting out: its credit bars sit close to the truth, A gets its share, and D and E get almost nothing, at every delay. Switch the target to team reacts to grade it on the truth it is built for. Ours: coalition estimator finds the group {A, B, C} in the coalition panel.
  • MAPPO. Its read-out is a shared advantage, so every agent's row is identical. Its leakage and missed credit are fixed by construction and are not evidence about MAPPO.
  • Coalitions. The true value comes from one three-agent coalition {A, B, C}. VDN and QMIX's mixer games put almost all of their mass on single agents, because a per-step game cannot express a coalition whose members act at different times.
