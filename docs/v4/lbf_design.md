# Part B design: how the LBF test was chosen (exploratory, seeds 0–4)

Everything here is pilot work on seeds 0–4, done **before** the Part B pre-registration was frozen. It records why the confirmatory design is what it is, including the designs that failed. The full pilot table at the end is generated by `scripts/v4/lbf_pilot_table.py` from `results/v4/lbf_*`.

## What was being asked

Does credit by persistent intervention, with exact truth, carry over from the hand-built coalition gridworld to a standard MARL benchmark, Level-Based Foraging (LBF; Papoudakis et al., 2021, the EPyMARL benchmark suite), where every episode has a **new random layout** (positions, agent levels, food levels)? Truth needs exact counterfactual continuations. So LBF was re-implemented deterministically and tested step for step against `lbforaging` 2.0.0 (decision E5). The team is a scripted greedy coalition planner (nearest agents until their level sum covers the food; it re-plans every step), so truth is exact and does not depend on a learned policy.

## Step 1: the first design failed, and why

The first design reused the v3 defaults: no-op null, package food rule, ε = 0.2, 200 training layouts, 200 stops. Both critics stayed at the equal split (set 0.86, MLP 1.05, equal split 0.91). With ten times the data the set critic still scored 0.84. Three causes were found, in order:

1. **The state hid the feasibility signal** (E6). Food levels were divided by the episode's largest food, so the critic could not tell whether two agents can lift a food. Fixed (one absolute level scale, plus each food's reward share); this alone did not help much (0.77–0.89).
2. **The no-op truth was dominated by planner deadlock** (E7). Under no-op, teammates are not told, so the greedy planner keeps assigning the frozen agent to food. Its partners wait beside the food forever. Removing one agent often costs *all* the food (true LOO = 10 for several agents at once). That is a property of the planner's blind spot, a discontinuous function of the greedy assignment, not of the agent's contribution.
3. **The removal truth was degenerate** (E7). The package caps food levels at the sum of the 3 lowest agent levels. With 4 agents, any 3 can lift any food, so with a generous horizon removal LOO is exactly zero. With a short horizon (H = 20) it is nonzero but mostly luck (luck floor 0.69–0.84 of a maximum 2).

## Step 2: the design that tests the question

**Heavy food under the removal null** (`food_cap="all"`: food levels up to the sum of *all* agent levels, a constructor option of the package). Now a heavy food needs specific agents, so who is **pivotal** for which food is the credit question. It is the core LBF mechanic, and removal truth is exact, non-degenerate (true LOO = 0 for 46% of agents) and mostly structural (luck floor 0.03–0.10 at ε = 0.2).

ε stays at 0.2, the v3 default used for every v3 cell. At ε = 0.05 the critics were worse (0.58 vs 0.18 on seeds 0–1), consistent with less diverse training states, but ε was not tuned on the result.

## Step 3: candidate improvements, tested before the freeze

- **Reward-decomposed critic** (E9; Hybrid Reward Architecture, van Seijen et al., 2017). LBF's return is a sum over food items, so V(s) = Σ_k value_k · P(food k collected | s) exactly. A per-food head on the two-set encoder gets one binary target per food instead of one sum. Pilot (5 seeds): 4 agents 0.34 vs set 0.30; 6 agents 0.89 vs 0.99. Mixed, within seed noise. Not made primary; kept as a secondary method because it is principled and cheap.
- **More data** (800 layouts, 800 stops, 6,000 steps): on seeds 0–1 the set critic went from 0.18 to 0.11 (floor 0.05). See the table.
